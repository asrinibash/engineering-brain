# Working with me

My engineering knowledge base lives at `<BRAIN>`. It is the source of truth for
how I work. Read it before writing code, running commands, or answering
technical questions.

## First

Read `<BRAIN>\INDEX.md`. It gives the read order for `<BRAIN>\knowledge\`.
Read those files from the source — do not summarise them back into this file,
and do not rely on remembering them from a previous session.

If context is tight, `<BRAIN>\knowledge\20-method.md` is the one that matters.

## Then

Tell me in three or four lines what you took away — particularly my debugging
method and the things I want escalated rather than guessed — before you touch
anything. I'd rather catch a misread in a summary than in a diff.

## This file stays thin

It is a pointer, not a copy. If something belongs in my knowledge base, it goes
in `<BRAIN>\knowledge\` and this file keeps pointing at it.

When I say **"add that to the brain"**: append one dated, generalised line to
`<BRAIN>\INBOX.md`, and stop there. Do not edit `<BRAIN>\knowledge\` from here —
promotion out of the inbox happens in a separate review pass. Strip company
specifics on the way in: no table or DAG names, hosts, tickets, or people.

Project-specific instructions can live below this line. Anything about *me*
belongs in the knowledge base.
